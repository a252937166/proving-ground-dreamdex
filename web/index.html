Built on dreamDEX.

Not around it: every read and every order goes through the venue's own contracts and tools.

dreamDEX is a fully onchain central limit order book on Somnia, open to apps built on top of it. Margin Rescue reads accounts through the venue's own bank, pools and SDK, and acts only through the operator grant dreamDEX already offers to bots. Nothing of the venue is forked, wrapped or re-hosted.

One account, every market

1,203 of 7,677 accounts held two or more.

On dreamDEX, every position in an account draws on the same USDso collateral. A move in BTC changes how much margin is left for ETH, SOL and the rest, and a short can be the leg that keeps a long alive.

So the question is not which position is losing the most, but which concrete reduction restores the buffer for the whole account under the move you fear. In the fork example the instinctive fix, cutting the larger BTC leg, needs three quarters of it; halving ETH does the job with less exposure removed.

Margin Rescue checks every leg, every resting order and every stop, and says when its answer cannot be trusted.

Questions

Short answers. The guide has the long ones.

Does Margin Rescue hold a key or sign anything?

No. The site and the app read public chain data only. A reduction is signed in your own terminal, with your own wallet, from a plan you review; the browser never sees a key.

Is it part of dreamDEX?

No. It is an independent tool built on dreamDEX's perps testnet: it uses the venue's contracts, SDK and operator grant as they are, and is not affiliated with or endorsed by dreamDEX or Somnia.

Which network does it run on?

dreamDEX perps on the Somnia Hideki testnet, chain 50383. Any other chain is refused, and nothing touches mainnet or real funds.

What does an analysis read?

The whole account at one pinned block: the bank's collateral and health, every position in all 21 pools, resting orders, the stop list and the BTC and ETH books. Then the block hash is checked again, so the snapshot cannot mix two blocks. That is why it takes 15 to 25 seconds.

What if the answer cannot be trusted?

Then there is no recommendation. A stale mark, an inexact reconciliation, a stop list the indexer has not caught up on, or a linked child account blocks it, and the reason is shown.

Has it run on the live network?

The read-only parts have: four real accounts reconcile exactly, and 7,700 of 7,700 holders match the bank's rounding. The live write, one guarded reduction on the project's own test account, is approved and has not run yet. The dashboard shows its status as it changes.
